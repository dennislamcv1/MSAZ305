Question 12

The single highest-risk assumption is that the whole solution can run inside EU regions end to end: that the existing Dataverse environment and Azure Data Lake Storage already sit in an EU region, and that every service and deployment type selected (Document Intelligence custom models, Azure AI Language, Azure AI Speech neural voices in French, German and Dutch, Azure AI Search, and a Lightweight Azure OpenAI model on Data Zone (EU) provisioned capacity) is available there. It carries the most downstream risk because GDPR applies at the infrastructure level to four of the five workflows (A, C, D and E), and later decisions are built on it: the service choices in Questions 4 and 5, the PTU tier for the two-second SLA in Question 8 and the $1.37-per-month cost projection in Question 9 all assume EU availability at those rates, whereas the other risks, such as cleaning the policy library for Workflow B or the consistent form layout for Workflow A, stay inside a single workflow. If the assumption is wrong, for example if the Lightweight model is not offered on provisioned capacity in an EU data zone or Dataverse turns out to be hosted outside the EU, Meridian would face either a patient-data migration before any workflow can go live, or a forced switch in Workflow D to the Frontier tier (about 16.7 times the token cost) or to consumption capacity that cannot guarantee two seconds, invalidating the cost projection the steering committee is asked to approve. Before procurement, I would verify the Dataverse and Data Lake regions, confirm availability of every chosen service and deployment type in a single EU region, re-price the cost model at EU Data Zone and PTU rates, and run a short proof of concept in that region measuring Workflow D's response time on a sample of claim emails against the two-second SLA, with Meridian's Data Protection Officer signing off the data flows.
